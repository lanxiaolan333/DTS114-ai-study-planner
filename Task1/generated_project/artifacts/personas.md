## Persona 1: Time-Pressed Undergraduate Student (Primary User)
**Profile:** 19–23, full-time student taking 4–6 modules; juggling classes, part-time work, and deadlines.  
**Responsibilities:**
- Keep up with weekly lectures, readings, labs, and assignments across modules
- Allocate study time effectively around fixed commitments
- Track progress toward deadlines and exam prep
**Needs:**
- Fast input and clear, realistic weekly plan based on available hours and deadlines
- Prioritisation across modules by difficulty and urgency
- Flexibility to adjust plan when schedule changes (missed sessions, extra time)
- Simple progress tracking and reminders of next actions

## Persona 2: Final-Year / Dissertation Student (Goal-Driven Planner)
**Profile:** 21–26, working toward a major project/dissertation plus remaining modules; high stakes and longer deadlines.  
**Responsibilities:**
- Break down long-term goals into weekly milestones (research, writing, revision)
- Balance deep work with ongoing coursework and assessments
- Maintain consistent progress to avoid last-minute rush
**Needs:**
- Milestone-based planning tied to learning goals (e.g., “master topic X”, “finish draft”)
- Plans that include focus blocks and revision cycles (spaced repetition)
- Visibility of workload distribution over weeks leading to deadline
- Ability to set and refine goals as scope changes

## Persona 3: Academic Support Tutor / Module Leader (Secondary Stakeholder)
**Profile:** Staff member supporting student study skills; wants practical, consistent planning advice.  
**Responsibilities:**
- Guide students in time management and independent learning strategies
- Help students prioritise tasks across modules and deadlines
- Monitor whether students are following a sustainable study routine
**Needs:**
- Confidence that plans are pedagogically sensible (balanced, not overloaded)
- Explanations/justifications for allocations (why this topic/time split)
- Ability for students to share/export plans for review and feedback
- Basic safeguards (avoid unsafe advice, encourage breaks, realistic pacing)
